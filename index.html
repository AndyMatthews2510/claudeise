What you will do

You join a team of four from different engineering majors. Together you choose a real engineering problem and turn it into a venture or a competition entry. Each week adds one piece of the project, and each class ends with something you can show.

Across the course you will:

  • run and record team meetings
  • write a business or competition proposal
  • negotiate with a supplier, client, co-founder or investor
  • write a scholarship statement, a CV and a cover letter
  • take a mock job interview
  • pitch your venture to a panel

You already have presentation and writing skills from your first two English courses, so class time goes to persuading, interacting and professional writing, not basic slides or paragraphs.

How each class runs

Every class follows the same five steps, so you always know what comes next. The bar shows the 150 minutes.

Week by week

Open a week to see what you will do, the language you will practise and what you hand in. Dark tags show graded tasks and their weight.

Team and meetings

Weeks 1 to 3. Form your team, then learn to run and record meetings in English.

Week 1Launch: engineer to entrepreneur

What you will do

Meet your team, find a real engineering problem worth solving, and pitch your idea in 60 seconds.

Language you will practise

Business terms, set 1: market, stakeholder, value, cost.

What you hand in

Diagnostic speaking task. Team formed.

Week 2Meetings I: taking part

What you will do

Role-play meetings with a given agenda and analyse a recorded meeting.

Language you will practise

Opening and closing, interrupting, clarifying, agreeing and disagreeing.

What you hand in

Meeting skills checklist.

Week 3Meetings II: leading and recordingTask 1: 10%

What you will do

Hold a team meeting to choose your venture idea, then write the minutes and action points.

Language you will practise

Chairing, summarising, action-point language.

What you hand in

Team meeting simulation, with minutes.

Business and proposals

Weeks 4 to 8. Learn business terms, then write the proposals your team and you will submit.

Week 4Business terms and models

What you will do

Build a business model canvas for your venture. Sort and match vocabulary in games.

Language you will practise

Business terms, set 2: revenue, margin, break-even, IP, funding.

What you hand in

Completed business model canvas.

Week 5Anatomy of a proposal

What you will do

Analyse real proposals, then draft the executive summary for your own.

Language you will practise

Problem, solution and benefit. Hedging and certainty.

What you hand in

Executive summary draft.

Week 6Persuasion and evidence

What you will do

Build your case with data, risk and a link to a Sustainable Development Goal. Critique each other's work.

Language you will practise

Persuasive structures. Ethos, logos and pathos.

What you hand in

Proposal outline with an evidence plan.

Week 7Scholarship proposal

What you will do

Study model statements, plan and draft your own, then get feedback in a peer clinic.

Language you will practise

Strengths, goals and impact without exaggeration.

What you hand in

Scholarship statement draft.

Week 8Competition proposal workshopTask 2: 20%Task 3: 10%

What you will do

Draft and revise your team proposal in class, then review another team's.

Language you will practise

Formal register, cohesion, concision.

What you hand in

Team proposal and scholarship statement.

Negotiation

Weeks 9 to 11. Practise reaching agreements, from short games to a recorded simulation.

Week 9Negotiation I: principles

What you will do

Play short negotiation games. Learn interests versus positions, and work out your BATNA.

Language you will practise

Proposing, countering, conceding, conditionals.

What you hand in

Negotiation preparation sheet.

Week 10Negotiation II: simulations

What you will do

Role-play supplier, client, co-founder and investor scenarios, and swap roles.

Language you will practise

Softening, diplomacy, closing a deal.

What you hand in

Feedback notes on a peer's performance.

Week 11Negotiation III: cross-cultural practiceTask 4: 15%

What you will do

Record a negotiation. Discuss an ethical dilemma and cultural norms in business.

Language you will practise

Tone, indirectness, ethical judgment.

What you hand in

Negotiation simulation, recorded.

Careers and pitch

Weeks 12 to 14. Present yourself for jobs, then pitch your venture to a panel.

Week 12Showcasing yourself

What you will do

Write achievement statements, build a CV and a cover letter, and edit with a peer.

Language you will practise

Action verbs, achievement statements, concise profiles.

What you hand in

CV and cover letter draft.

Week 13Job interviewsTask 5: 20%

What you will do

Take mock panel interviews in rotation, answering with the STAR method.

Language you will practise

Behavioural answers, strengths, handling difficult questions.

What you hand in

CV, cover letter and mock interview.

Week 14Final pitch and reflectionTask 6: 20%

What you will do

Pitch your venture to a panel, answer questions and negotiate a short term. Then reflect on the course.

Language you will practise

All strands together. Thinking on your feet.

What you hand in

Final pitch, Q&A and negotiation. Learning log.

How your work is marked

One rubric covers every spoken and written task. It has four criteria, and each is scored from 1 to 4. The four scores are added up and converted to the weight of the task. Fluency and pronunciation are not separate criteria: fluency counts under organisation and interaction, and pronunciation counts under language.

CriterionWhat we look for
Task fulfilmentIn speaking: The purpose is achieved: the panel is persuaded, a deal is reached, the question is answered.In writing: The genre's purpose and required content are met.
Language range and accuracyIn speaking: Business vocabulary, grammar control, and clear pronunciation, stress and intonation.In writing: Business vocabulary, grammar, spelling and punctuation.
Organisation and interactionIn speaking: Fluent flow, signposting, turn-taking, and responding to questions and interruptions.In writing: Logical structure, headings, paragraphing and cohesion.
Professionalism and audience awarenessIn speaking: Polite, confident delivery, suitable register, eye contact and posture.In writing: Suitable register and tone, professional layout and conventions.

What you will be able to do

  1. Use business and professional terms correctly in engineering contexts.
  2. Take part effectively in meetings, negotiations and teamwork.
  3. Present your qualifications, skills and strengths clearly in applications and interviews.
  4. Write structured proposals for business, academic and engineering purposes.
  5. Persuade an audience with a clear argument and evidence.
  6. Adapt what you say and write to professional, academic and cross-cultural audiences.

The course aims at CEFR B2+ moving towards C1.

Every venture serves a global goal

Your team's venture must address at least one of these Sustainable Development Goals.

  • SDG 17: Partnerships for the GoalsCollaboration and negotiation across teams and cultures.
